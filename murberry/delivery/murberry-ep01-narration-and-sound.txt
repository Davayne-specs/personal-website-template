PART 1  -  FOR THE VOICE ACTOR
=====================================================================

WHO MURBERRY IS
Murberry is the show and the voice. He is never seen. Think of a
nature-documentary narrator who has seen a thousand bodies and is not
surprised by yours. He explains, he never cheers.

HOW HE SOUNDS
- Unhurried, warm, low, certain. About 130-140 words a minute.
- Close to the mic, as if talking to one person in bed at 11pm.
  Not a "voice-over announcer" read. No sell, no hype.
- Respectful about the body. Never pitying, never jokey.
- Let the full stops land. One idea per sentence, a breath between.
- No upward inflection at the ends of lines. Statements, not questions.
- Do NOT add words, "let's go", laughs or exclamation energy.

TECHNICAL
- WAV, 48 kHz, 24-bit, mono. Dry: no music, no reverb, no compression
  or EQ baked in. Quiet room, pop filter.
- Record each numbered line as its own take, 2 good takes per line,
  with 2 seconds of silence before and after. Name files by line
  number, e.g. EP01_L07_take1.wav.
- Also record 10 seconds of room tone.
- Each line has a WINDOW (start to end). The read should fit inside it.
  Finishing a little early is fine; running long is not. If a line
  will not fit naturally, read it naturally anyway and flag it - the
  edit can breathe.

PRONUNCIATION
- pectoralis major ......... PECK-tor-AL-iss  MAY-jer
- anterior deltoid ......... an-TEER-ee-er  DEL-toyd
- triceps .................. TRY-seps
- serratus anterior ........ seh-RAY-tus  an-TEER-ee-er
- actin .................... ACK-tin
- myosin ................... MY-oh-sin
- ATP ...................... A. T. P.  (spell it out)
- insulin .................. IN-suh-lin
- sixty-six ................ say it in full, not "six-six"
- fibre .................... FY-ber

PART 2  -  FOR THE SOUND DESIGN / MIX CREW
=====================================================================

WHAT YOU ARE MIXING
- Narration (Part 1) on top. Nothing competes with the voice.
- A temp sound bed already exists (synthesized in code, in the
  episode's current master). Use it as a guide for timing and feel,
  or replace any layer with your own recorded foley. Recorded paper
  will very likely sound better than the temp folds.
- Captions stay burned into the picture, so the voice must stay in
  sync with the timecodes in Part 1 (within about +/- 0.2 s).

SOUND IDENTITY (from the brand sheet)
- Scene fold: a deep paper fold, slowed down and pitched down so it has
  weight - almost like a breath. Real paper, recorded close, then
  slowed 2x and pitched down, with a short soft room.
- Bed: a soft, warm drone or pad, barely there. The temp bed is a D sus2
  chord that warms toward D major in Beat 5 and resolves at the end.
- Load: a subtle low hum that rises as the muscles turn red and fades as
  they rest. Make sure it has upper harmonics (110-220 Hz); phones do
  not play 55 Hz.
- No music with a beat, no whooshes, no risers, no stingers, no "UI" clicks.

LEVELS / DELIVERY
- Loudness target: -14 LUFS integrated (social), true peak -1 dBTP.
- Dialogue around -16 to -18 LUFS short-term; pad 18-22 dB under the voice.
- Duck the pad and hum about 4-6 dB under narration, slow attack/release
  (300 ms / 800 ms) so the ducking is never heard.
- Folds can sit up to the voice level in the gaps, and 6-8 dB under it
  when a line is playing.
- Deliver: full mix WAV 48 kHz 24-bit stereo, plus stems
  (DIALOGUE / FOLEY / BED / HUM), all starting at 00:00.0, 2:30.0 long.
